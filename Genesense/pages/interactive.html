How Well Do You Know GMOs?

10 questions. 5 minutes. See what you actually understand about genetic modification, health, and ethics.

This quiz tests your knowledge of what we've covered on GeneSense. You'll get instant feedback on every answer—whether you're right or wrong, you'll learn something.

No score counting against you. Just knowledge.

Question 1 of 10
What does GMO stand for?
A) Genetically Modified Organism
B) Generally Modified Organism
C) Genetically Managed Organism
D) Genetic Market Optimization

✓ Correct. GMO stands for Genetically Modified Organism—an organism whose DNA has been deliberately altered using biotechnology.

Question 2 of 10
How long have GMO crops been commercially available?
A) Since the 1960s
B) Since the 1980s
C) Since the mid-1990s
D) Since 2010

✓ Correct. GeneSense explains that GMO crops have been grown commercially since the mid-1990s. See the “Introduction to GMOs” section in the Articles and Human Health pages.

Question 3 of 10
What is Golden Rice designed to address?
A) Pest resistance in crops
B) Vitamin A deficiency
C) Soil contamination
D) Climate change adaptation

✓ Correct. Golden Rice is engineered to produce beta-carotene (which the body converts to vitamin A). Vitamin A deficiency affects millions globally, particularly in developing countries.

Question 4 of 10
What do major scientific organizations conclude about GMO food safety?
A) GMOs are completely risk-free
B) Approved GMO foods are as safe as conventional crops
C) GMOs are inherently dangerous
D) GMO safety is still unknown

✓ Correct. The paper summarizes WHO (2014) and NASEM (2016): evidence has not shown approved GMO foods to be less safe than conventional counterparts. Assessment must still be product-specific, and continued research and oversight remain important. See Human Health and References.

Question 5 of 10
Which ethical issue is NOT typically part of the GMO debate?
A) Consumer right to know/GMO labeling
B) Corporate power and patents
C) The color of vegetable packaging
D) Farmer autonomy and seed-saving rights

✓ Correct. GeneSense discusses labeling and consumer choice, patents and corporate control, and farmer autonomy. Packaging color is not one of the ethical issues reviewed. See Ethics and Society.

Question 6 of 10
What is Bt technology used for in GMO crops?
A) Making crops grow faster
B) Increasing nutritional content
C) Providing natural pest resistance
D) Improving soil quality

✓ Correct. Bt is short for Bacillus thuringiensis, a soil bacterium whose genes can help crops resist specific insect pests. GeneSense discusses Bt crops in Human Health, Articles, and Case Studies; outcomes depend on crop and context.

Question 7 of 10
Which region has mandatory GMO labeling?
A) The United States
B) The European Union
C) Most developing countries
D) Australia only

✓ Correct. GeneSense explains that the European Union requires GMO labeling under its rules, while regulatory approaches differ by region. See Ethics and the Society article on access and labeling.

Question 8 of 10
What is a key ethical concern about GMO patents?
A) They prevent farmers from saving and replanting seeds
B) They make seeds more expensive
C) They reduce farmer autonomy
D) All of the above

✓ Correct. GeneSense describes concerns that patents may restrict seed-saving, increase seed costs, and reduce farmer autonomy. See Ethics and Society.

Question 9 of 10
How do GMO crops potentially impact food security?
A) Higher yields could feed more people on less land
B) Climate-resistant crops could help adapt to changing conditions
C) But over-reliance on few varieties creates vulnerability
D) All of the above

✓ Correct. GeneSense presents potential benefits such as improved yields and adaptation to some growing conditions, alongside concerns such as resistance and reduced crop diversity. Food security also depends on access, affordability, and wider social conditions. See Society and the related articles.

Question 10 of 10
What's the main message of GeneSense?
A) GMOs are always safe and should be everywhere
B) GMOs are dangerous and should be banned
C) GMOs are a tool with benefits and concerns that deserve critical thinking
D) GMOs don't matter to society

✓ Correct. GeneSense presents GMOs as a technology with potential benefits and risks. It emphasizes evaluating evidence, product-specific safety, ethical concerns, environmental effects, and social impacts rather than treating all GMOs as uniformly good or bad.
